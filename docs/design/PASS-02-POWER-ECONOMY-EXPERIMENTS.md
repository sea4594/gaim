# GAIM — Mechanics research, Pass 02: make stored energy matter

**Status:** Three independent experimental rulesets, NOT adopted into production. **Baseline:** `charge-loom/v0.1`, all seven golden scenario witnesses unchanged. **Date:** 2026-10-22. **Code:** `experiments/`, with tests and deterministic survey. **Design question:** Does the energy generated by successful storm collisions enable worthwhile further decisions, instead of being only a win counter?

## 1. What was actually built

Three playable, pure-Python opt-in mechanical branches use the same nine rounds, three lanes, four shared edits, stored energy cap of three per lane, three coolant, hull, waves, and v0.1 end target. Each energy expenditure uses **one of the four shared edits**, costs **one already-stored capacitor energy**, and can be used at most once per round. The normal three edits (SHIFT, HOLD, DAMP) remain legal. These tests **do not** change the network server or shipping engine.

### Trial A: BARRIER — emergency survival

* Action `('barrier', lane)`; spend one energy from that lane's capacitor.
* Block up to two unmatched damage **from that lane this resolution**, including both regular breach and end-of-round-nine residual charge leakage, in that order. Unused protection expires immediately.
* No new income or future effect. There is no automatic charge/repair and no stacking.
* Hard choice: preserve energy toward the win target or spend it to avert hull loss; an attack blocked from lane A cannot be blocked by purchasing from lane B.
* Weakness: overlap with DAMP and lack of productive feedback. This may simply add a panic button.

### Trial B: RELAY — positional flexibility

* Action `('relay', token_id)`; token must currently occupy LEFT or RIGHT, whose capacitor must hold at least one energy.
* Spend one energy in that **source** lane and move the token directly to the opposite outer lane (two lanes instead of normal one); takes a single edit.
* Hard choice: expend score to repair an imminent spatial mismatch versus making separate shifts or accepting damage.
* Weakness: narrowly useful and may primarily shortcut an existing movement puzzle, rather than create a strategic economy.

### Trial C: CAPACITOR BRIDGE — temporary engine-building

* Action `('link', source_lane, destination_lane)`; source/destination **must be adjacent**. Spend one stored energy in source. Costs one edit.
* For **the current resolution and the next resolution**, whenever at least one positive/negative pair cancels in the source lane, the adjacent destination gets **one extra energy**, subject to its cap of three. The source still earns ordinary energy. This is one bonus per round with a qualifying collision, **not one bonus per matched strength or pair**.
* At most one bridge can exist team-wide. It cannot be replaced early; it expires after two resolutions or game over. Spending must be affordable before the round begins—power generated by this round's matches cannot retroactively pay for the link.
* Hard choice: invest score in a place where storms can be converted, anticipating one or two profitable beats; the eventual energy is redistributed to a different lane, which changes the value of routing charges.
* Risk: if a player can buy a bridge on the exact turn of a known collision, this may become a routine arithmetic optimization rather than a meaningful forecast. It may also cheapen the lane-cap balance rule. Thus this is a **leading experiment**, not an approved v0.2 rule.

## 2. Verified counterexamples: spending can win OR lose

These are reproducible **single-state mechanical witnesses**, not arbitrary descriptive hypotheticals. The tests verify outcomes exactly.

| Mechanic | Spending is right | Spending is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| BARRIER | At 1 hull, capacitors `[3,3,2]`, a left-lane +1, −1, −2 breach. Barrier absorbs 2 unmatched damage; the paired charge refills the 1 spent from LEFT. **Win**, while passing loses on hull. | At 5 hull, `[2,3,2]`, a right-lane +1/−1 already makes the target. An unnecessary center BARRIER reduces remaining power to 7. **Loss**, while passing wins. |
| RELAY | At 2 hull, `[3,3,2]`, left +1 and right −1 are breaching. One relay spends LEFT power to move +1 directly to RIGHT, creates a match, and ends `[2,3,3]`. **Win**, while a normal one-lane shift still loses. | At 6 hull, `[3,3,2]`, an unrelated far-left +1 will leak at the deadline; passing survives with target intact. Relay consumes one energy without creating a match. **Loss by target**. |
| BRIDGE | At the start of round 8, `[3,2,0]`, two center opposite-charge pairs will breach in consecutive rounds. A center→right bridge costs 1 center energy, adds 1 RIGHT on each successful round, and completes `[3,3,2]`. **Win**, while no bridge ends `[3,3,0]` and loses. | At round 9, `[2,3,2]`, the only imminent match is on RIGHT. An unnecessary CENTER→RIGHT bridge spends center energy, earns no center bonus, and turns a normal win into **loss by target**. |

The two-resolution BRIDGE example shows the potential for a productive loop. However, it is constructed; a representative nine-round human game has not been demonstrated to routinely require it.

## 3. Systematic quantitative tests

### A. Full nine-round naive-greedy simulation

`python3 -m experiments.power_economy --seeds 24`

The same 24 fixed baseline wave seeds (0–23) were played independently in each mode by the same simple, one-round hill-climbing heuristic. Results:

| Rule | Wins / 24 | Survived | Uses of new power move | Mean stored power at finish |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original v0.1 | 8 | 8 | — | 5.125 |
| BARRIER | 8 | 9 | 10 | 5.250 |
| RELAY | 8 | 9 | 11 | 5.333 |

This simplistic policy is **not optimal** and does not even evaluate multi-round BRIDGE opportunities; BRIDGE is intentionally omitted from this table. These results provide **no evidence** that BARRIER or RELAY improves full-game win rate. The policy treats hull preservation and energy accumulation with arbitrary weights, and the 24 scenarios are not a representative human challenge set.

### B. Exhaustive final-round microstate survey

`python3 -m experiments.finale_survey --cases 1000`

Exactly 1,000 deterministic, synthetic pre-final-round states were generated with 2–3 charges already in NOW, random small strengths/signs/lanes, hull 1–5, stored energy 1–3 per lane, and coolant 0–3. Each ruleset enumerated all valid combinations of token edits plus its special edit, subject to the four-edit budget, one edit per token, maximum one spending action, and coolant availability. A state was considered winnable if **at least one legal one-round action plan** ended in a win.

| Ruleset | Winnable out of 1,000 | Extra solvable relative to original | Winning plans using spend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original v0.1 | 214 | — | — |
| BARRIER | 226 | 12 | 1,632 |
| RELAY | 218 | 4 | 166 |
| BRIDGE | 247 | 33 | 2,609 |

**What these numbers mean:** All three spending abilities preserve the no-spend solutions and can create new winning plans. BRIDGE changes the most of these sampled, exact terminal situations. These 1,000 states are **not real wavebook states**, and “winnable” is **not a human success rate**. Winning-plan totals count many equivalent or nearly equivalent plans; do not use them as replayability estimates. In particular, the final-round survey cannot reveal the value of BRIDGE's extra *future* resolution.

On these sampled states, BARRIER adds 12, RELAY 4, and BRIDGE 33 winnable cases. The main inference is that **temporary cross-lane power generation appears a more fertile resource engine** than paying to prevent a little damage or move one token faster, within the current scoring rules.

## 4. Judgment and next experiment

**Do not simply add all three buttons.** Three special actions would clutter portrait play, worsen analysis paralysis, and obscure what caused any increase in depth. The immediate defensive BARRIER and long SHIFT RELAY provide exact interesting corner cases but little broader evidence for improved strategy. Retain them as controls; don't merge them into the main game yet.

**Promote BRIDGE as the lead research direction only.** The prospect of spending scarce energy to make future collisions more valuable offers a real economy: match storms → generate power → invest some power → set up future matches → gain energy where it is scarce. That can create interlocking decisions across turns, and LINK allocation could create meaningful cooperative authorship. But it needs more proof and perhaps substantial redesign:

1. **Temporal commitment:** Compare current+next BRIDGE against delayed next+following BRIDGE. Ask whether decisions require genuinely looking ahead, rather than buying a guaranteed bonus when a collision is already imminent. Avoid scenario “clairvoyance”: players must know enough to forecast realistically.
2. **Different objectives:** Revisit the 8-of-9 cap win condition. Test public missions whose relative resource priorities shift. An ability that wins by moving power to the weakest lane could otherwise become an automatic purchase whenever center production overflows.
3. **Productive risk:** Measure the value of burning a point from an almost-full lane, not just making a point appear elsewhere. Investigate a version that can *fail and incur a liability*, not just waste the cost, but don't introduce opaque random punishment.
4. **True cooperative design:** Stress two rival structures: (a) separate ownership of timing versus energy investment and (b) constrained contributions to a collective plan. Evaluate actual decision authorship with humans. An action combo between two slots alone is not player agency.
5. **Stronger testing:** Use full-foresight search plus limited-info planners and human co-op sessions. A 24-seed one-step greedy evaluation and one-round synthetic exhaustive enumeration are insufficient to establish long-term depth, fairness or fun.

## 5. Current project status and how to reproduce

The live rules remain `charge-loom/v0.1`. New files live under `experiments/`, `tests/`, and `docs/design/`; the only existing file changed is `scripts/release_gate.sh`, which now syntax-compiles `experiments/` and discovers their tests alongside the original suites.

```bash
bash scripts/release_gate.sh
python3 -m experiments.power_economy --seeds 24
python3 -m experiments.finale_survey --cases 1000
```

No mobile interface was added; no 1–4-player online rules were silently modified. New rules enter the production engine **only after** stronger experiments confirm that they create better decisions and improve play.

## Reference design comparison

Complex co-op board games such as *Spirit Island* and *Aeon's End* make energy and other resources useful as **expenditure decisions**: players must decide when to invest in stronger future options versus preserve the resources needed for current threats. This is a relevant general design pattern, not a proposal to copy their implementation. See [Spirit Island rulebook](https://www.boardgamesbot.com/assets/games-library/spirit-island/manual_eng.pdf) and [Aeon's End rules](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules).
