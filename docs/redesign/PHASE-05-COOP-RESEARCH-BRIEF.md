# GAIM Phase 5 handoff — cooperative decision architecture

**Architecture to test first:** Storm Engine, with its tactical charge grammar and persistent settlement. Storm Operations and Forecast Market remain live controls. Do **not** incorporate Operations' entire card/missions subsystem into Engine.

## Three competing one-rule-family experiments

A. **Seat-owned interventions** — players have 2 visible, personal tactical capabilities that change over time; choose at most one action each in a round. Team budget currently permits two paid edits, so explicitly test whether non-acting seats get meaningful responsibility; do not disguise inactivity with cosmetic choices. Compare open permissions, per-seat rotating ownership, and an alternate two-edit/strategic-resource design.

B. **Two-part infrastructure commitments** — construction itself is jointly authored: one seat proposes the investment, another allocates the power source/sacrifice. To count as two independent choices, the *same proposal must sometimes succeed or fail differently depending on the other player's allocation*, and vice versa. Avoid adding a voting mini-game where all choices are obvious. Solo should choose both inputs in one interface, without extra avatars.

C. **Conditional cooperative plans** — two seats jointly fund one contingent move with an explicit public condition. Specify the trigger timing, resource payment, action ordering, deadline behavior, and which costs are lost on an unused branch. Compare against a plain unconditional action with equivalent total cost.

## Gate measurements

* Two independently valuable contributions demonstrated per tested mechanism in replayable, legal state transitions.
* No model is accepted merely because it increases the proportion of successful games.
* Under 3–4 players, report how often someone cannot make or influence a consequential decision, separating deliberate passing from lacking an opportunity.
* Test human sessions with free communication, including a strong player among novices; do not rely on enforced secrecy or arbitrary hand withholding.
* Balanced 1–4-player threat scaling is a prerequisite for claiming fairness; current Operations `11/40` vs `37/40` Phase 3 win rate pattern is not acceptable evidence of balanced difficulty.
* Require the proposed mechanism to preserve at least two distinct long-range settlement plans and no >1 page of new core rules before polishing.

## Pivot trigger

If none of A, B, or C generates useful human cooperation without bloating rules, abandon Engine's current shared-action economy and explore a leaner Operations-style game with a persistent mission/resource axis. Do not transfer a failed card menu simply for perceived modern-board-game aesthetics.
